Think of an email as a **letter with an envelope**. The different fields tell the email system **who the message is primarily for, who should know about it, and who should not be visible to the other recipients**.

### 1. To

**To = the main recipient.**

Use it for the person who is expected to **read and/or act on the email**.

**Example:**

> To: [Manager@company.com](mailto:Manager@company.com)
> Subject: Weekly Report

You are directly sending the report to the manager.

**Think:**
**To = "This is for you."**

---

### 2. CC

**CC = Carbon Copy.**

The people in CC receive the email, but they are usually **not the primary person expected to act**.

**Example:**

> To: [Manager@company.com](mailto:Manager@company.com)
> CC: [Accountant@company.com](mailto:Accountant@company.com)

The manager is receiving the report. The accountant is copied so they **know what was sent**.

**Think:**
**CC = "You should know about this."**

Important: **Everyone can see who is in CC.**

---

### 3. BCC

**BCC = Blind Carbon Copy.**

BCC works like CC, except **other recipients cannot see the BCC recipients**.

**Example:**

> To: [Manager@company.com](mailto:Manager@company.com)
> BCC: [CEO@company.com](mailto:CEO@company.com)

The manager receives the email normally. The CEO also receives it, but the manager does **not see that the CEO was BCC'd**.

**Think:**
**BCC = "You should receive this, but others should not see that you received it."**

---

### The fundamental difference

| Field   | Purpose                         | Visible to other recipients? |
| ------- | ------------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| **To**  | Main recipient                  | Yes                          |
| **CC**  | Keep someone informed           | Yes                          |
| **BCC** | Keep someone informed privately | No                           |

### A simple office analogy

Imagine you are handing a document to your manager:

* **To:** You hand the document directly to the manager.
* **CC:** You give a visible copy to the manager's assistant.
* **BCC:** You secretly give another copy to someone else without the visible recipients knowing.

### One important email-management rule

**Do not use BCC simply because someone is important.** Its main purpose is **recipient privacy** or discreet distribution.

## 4. Reply vs Reply All

These determine **who receives your response**.

### Reply

**Reply = respond only to the person who sent the email.**

Example:

> John sends an email to you and Sarah.
> You click **Reply**.

Your response goes to **John only**.

**Think:**
**Reply = "I am responding to you."**

---

### Reply All

**Reply All = respond to everyone included in the conversation.**

Example:

> John → You + Sarah + David

If you click **Reply All**, your response goes to:

> John + Sarah + David

**Think:**
**Reply All = "Everyone involved needs to see my response."**

### The key difference

| Action        | Who receives your response?       |
| ------------- | --------------------------------- |
| **Reply**     | Sender                            |
| **Reply All** | Sender + other visible recipients |

**Virtual assistant rule:** Before clicking **Reply All**, ask:

> **"Does everyone on this email actually need to see my response?"**

If not, use **Reply**.

> **To:** Your own email
> **BCC:** The 100 recipients

This prevents those 100 people from seeing each other's email addresses.
